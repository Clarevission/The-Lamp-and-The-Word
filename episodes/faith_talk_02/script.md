## 1. Hook

[quiet, direct, like a late-night conversation]

There's a question a lot of people carry around and almost never say out loud.

If God is real, does he actually care about me?

Not humanity in general. Not the good, put-together people. Me. The one who drifted. The one who doubted, or made a mess of things, or never really believed in the first place.

Would God come looking for someone like me?

[beat]

Jesus answered that question with a story about a single sheep. Let's sit with one verse together.

## 2. The verse

[settled, warm]

In Luke chapter fifteen, the religious leaders were complaining that Jesus welcomed sinners, and even ate with them. So Jesus asked them a question.

[read slowly; the verse is on screen]

[NLT Luke 15:4: Dominique pastes exact text]

[pause]

It's phrased as a question, but Jesus isn't really asking for information. Everyone listening knew the answer. Of course you'd go after it. Any shepherd would. A sheep alone in open country is vulnerable. It can't defend itself, and it usually can't find its own way back. Leaving it out there isn't really an option for a good shepherd.

And that's his point. If you'd go to all that trouble for a sheep, why are you surprised that God would do it for a person?

In Sunday's episode, we walked through the whole story: the shepherd who searches, carries the sheep home on his shoulders, and calls his friends to celebrate. Today I want to stay with just this one verse, because inside it are three things that answer our question.

## 3. Three things

[teaching voice, clear; each "One / Two / Three" line matches an on-screen label]

One. The shepherd moves first.

Notice what the sheep does in this story. Nothing. It doesn't find its way back. It doesn't call for help. It doesn't clean itself up first. The shepherd notices it's missing, and he goes.

That matters, because a lot of us assume faith starts with us. Get your life together, and then maybe God will notice. The Bible tells it the other way around. God makes the first move. Centuries before Jesus, God promised through the prophet Ezekiel that he himself would go and search for his scattered sheep. In Jesus, that promise walks right into the room. And the apostle Paul says Christ died for us not after we got better, but while we were still sinners.

[beat]

Two. He searches until he finds it.

Not for a while. Not until it gets dark. Until he finds it.

There's no moment in this story where the shepherd shrugs and says, that one's wandered too far. Maybe you've wondered if you've gone past the point of no return. This verse doesn't leave room for that. The distance is real. But it isn't the shepherd's limit.

[beat]

Three. The one matters.

The shepherd still has ninety-nine. By the numbers, he's fine. But he doesn't think in percentages. He thinks in names.

You are not a statistic to God. You're not lost in a crowd of eight billion people. When Jesus told this story, he was looking at real faces, tax collectors and outsiders, and telling them, this is how God sees you. You are worth going after.

And when the shepherd finds that one sheep, Jesus says there's joy in heaven. Not a sigh. Not a lecture. Joy.

## 4. For you, and a prayer

[slower, personal]

So, does God really come looking for you?

Maybe you grew up around faith and slowly drifted. Maybe you've never prayed in your life. Either way, the answer is the same.

Yes. According to Jesus, he already has. Jesus said he came to seek and save the lost, and he went all the way to the cross to do it.

And being found doesn't mean you have to fix everything first. It can start with something as simple as stopping. Turning around. Letting yourself be picked up. In the story, the sheep doesn't walk home. It's carried. And the shepherd isn't irritated about it. He's overjoyed.

If that's where you are today, let's pray.

[prayer, unhurried]

Lord Jesus, thank you that you are the shepherd who comes looking. Some days I feel far from you, and some days I'm not even sure I want to be found. But you know exactly where I am. Thank you for coming after me. I'm done running. Carry me home, and help me believe that you're glad to. In your name, amen.
