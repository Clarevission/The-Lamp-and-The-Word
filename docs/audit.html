Findings

Ordered by risk. "Critical" items affect monetization or rights. "High" items cost views or time.

Critical

NLT permission doesn't cover monetized videos

Tyndale's online policy lets you quote NLT in videos, as long as each description credits it. It excludes recordings that are "commercial or monetized". The credit line is already in every description, but once the channel is in the Partner Program it needs Tyndale's written permission. The usual limit is 500 verses, and no more than 25% of a work.

Fill in Tyndale's Permissions Questionnaire now (tyndale.com/permissions, or permissions@tyndale.com). Describe the channel, how much text appears per video, and the plan to monetize. There may be a fee.

Critical

Suno rights depend on how each song was made

Commercial rights apply only to songs made while on Pro or Premier. Upgrading later does not cover songs made on the free plan. Since Sep 3, 2026, downloads are capped: Pro 20 a month, Premier 60. Suno output may not be copyrightable, so don't register it in Content ID.

Confirm s0 to s3 were made on a paid plan, and save screenshots of the plan and creation dates in Drive. Make a few new beds per series, so videos don't all share one soundtrack.

Critical

"Inauthentic content" is the main monetization risk for faceless AI channels

YouTube's July 2025 and July 2026 rules demonetize whole channels whose videos look template-made with little variation, including AI built from generic templates. Her own narration and distinct scripts are strong protection. A single reused look, intro and music bed works against it.

Keep her voice and perspective central, especially in the faith talks. Vary the scene style and music by series. Keep the title card short. Never batch-produce look-alike videos.

High

The "altered content: No" answer depends on the visuals staying clearly cartoon

Disclosure is only required for realistic-looking content. Since May 2026, YouTube labels detected photorealistic AI automatically. Faith Talk 01 uses stills for hands (T3), a church pew (P2) and prayer (P4). If those look like photos, "No" becomes the wrong answer.

Make those stills in the same Pixar-like style as the clips. If any shot looks real, answer "Yes" for that video; a label doesn't hurt reach. Also check that Flow exports carry no visible watermark.

High

Close to the "made for kids" line

YouTube names animated characters and storytelling as signals that a video is for kids, and it can override the setting. A kids designation turns off comments, end screens, personalized ads and Test & Compare. Animated content "that appeals to everyone" is fine.

Keep "Not made for kids". Keep titles, thumbnails and topics adult-facing (faith talks, prayer, real-life application). Avoid nursery-style music or wording.

High

Episode 1 went out on a Wednesday, and its Shorts were never posted

Oct 14, 2026 was a Wednesday, the faith-talk slot. The Shorts schedule ran Oct 15 to 23, so those dates have now passed with nothing posted. Shorts are the cheapest way to send new viewers to Episode 1 while it's fresh.

New plan: Shorts start Sat Oct 17, Faith Talk 01 goes out Wed Oct 21, and the Sunday rhythm starts Oct 25 with Episode 2. The full calendar is below and in calendar.ics.

High

Episode 1 loose ends

The handoff lists three open items: check the C2 thumbnail is live, pin the comment, and review the auto-captions. Auto-captions often get names like "Pharisees" wrong.

Pin the comment today. For captions, upload the script as a transcript with "Without timing" in Studio. YouTube syncs it to her voice and the result is accurate.

High

Advanced YouTube features aren't confirmed

Custom thumbnails, Test & Compare, and linking a Short to its full video all need phone verification (intermediate features) or advanced features. Test & Compare can now test up to 3 thumbnails and titles.

Studio, then Settings, then Channel, then Feature eligibility: verify by phone and request advanced features. Then link every Short to its full video.

Medium

Upload automation has a hard ceiling

Videos uploaded through a new, unaudited YouTube API project are locked to private, and TikTok's API does the same. Pinned comments and end screens have no API at all. Postiz would need its own developer apps and both audits.

Automate everything up to "ready to upload" (done). The upload is a 5-minute Studio step with a checklist, which also enforces "nothing public without her OK". Schedule Shorts with each platform's own scheduler: YouTube Studio on desktop, Instagram's "Schedule this Reel", and TikTok Studio (up to 10 days ahead).

Medium

The project lives on one laptop

The scripts, fonts and pipeline were in a Cowork folder. The shared Drive folder shows no files from Clarence's account, and nothing was in version control.

Done for the code: it now lives in the GitHub repo Clarevission/The-Lamp-and-The-Word. Media (recordings, Flow clips, music, masters) stays in the Drive folder; share its contents with both accounts. The repo can go back to private; Claude's access doesn't depend on it being public.

Medium

Single-pass loudness and silence trimming

One-pass loudnorm can miss -14 LUFS by 1 to 2 LU. Cutting silences outright can make delivery feel rushed.

Done: the toolkit measures first and then normalizes (two-pass). It shortens long pauses to 0.35 s instead of removing them.

Done

Brand, credit line and limits are enforced by the tools

The description builder refuses to finish without the full Tyndale credit line. It also checks YouTube's limits: title, 5,000-byte description, 500 tag characters, at most 15 hashtags, and chapters (at least 3, starting at 0:00, each 10 s or longer). Verse cards render only from verses.json, and Flow audio is always stripped.

What's automated now

The toolkit in the GitHub repo, tested end to end on a full sample episode. One command runs everything: python -m lampstudio all episodes/faith_talk_01.

The tools do

  • Clean each narration with her audio chain, then two-pass loudness
  • Time every shot to her voice, with no shot over 29 s; pin the verse card to the second she reads it
  • Animated title card, verse card (lines appear as she reads), part labels, end screen with Subscribe and Watch Next zones
  • Music bed: Suno tracks crossfaded, ducked to 0.15 under her voice and raised on intro and end
  • 1080p master at -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP, with fade in and out
  • Captions (.srt) and a transcript; optional Whisper
  • Description with chapters, tags, pinned comment, title variants
  • 3 thumbnail variants for Test & Compare
  • Vertical Shorts with brand captions and a "full story" end tag, plus a caption file for posting
  • QC report, upload folder and Studio checklist
  • Calendar of publish slots plus recording and clip deadlines (.ics)
  • 48-hour and 7-day check against the targets, from a Studio CSV

Dominique does

  • Approve scripts (drafts provided)
  • Record: one session, one WAV per section
  • Make Flow clips from the shot list's prompts
  • Paste the exact NLT text into verses.json
  • Watch the render and give notes
  • Click Schedule in Studio, Instagram and TikTok
  • Reply to comments and pray for requests

Never automated

  • Publishing without her OK
  • AI voice
  • Verse text typed from memory
  • Any form of fake engagement
